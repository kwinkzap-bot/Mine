How this scanner reads a crossover

Each symbol carries two intraday lines, both measured from the 9:15 open: the change in total call OI and the change in total put OI. A crossover is the moment those lines swap places. Puts pulling ahead is BULL — writers are stacking OI below the market. Calls pulling ahead is BEAR.

This is deliberately not a PCR reading. A symbol can sit at PCR 0.6 — heavily call-dominant — and still print a BULL cross, because the levels describe where the book already was and the change-lines describe where today's money went. Cross Count is how many times the lines have swapped today: a name in double digits is chopping, not trending, and its latest tag is worth very little.

Quality grades the two legs. On a BULL cross, puts are the winning leg: fresh put OI is aggressive, call OI leaving is covering, and both at once is strong — new money and exiting money agreeing on direction. Early crosses carry more weight than late-session ones, which are usually unwinds rather than fresh commitment.
